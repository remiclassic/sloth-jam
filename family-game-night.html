Family party games for kids and parents

A game night guide · October 2026

A good family game night needs a game the youngest can play and the oldest still enjoy, rounds short enough that nobody waits long, and losing that doesn’t sting. Sloth Jam is built around all three, on a single phone, tablet or computer.

How a family plays Sloth Jam

  • The Family Cup. Pass one device around the sofa. The game shows whose turn it is, and everyone plays for the cup.
  • Everyone gets a sloth. Each family member has their own sloth, and the whole family shares one closet of outfits.
  • One-minute games. Each of the 59 party games lasts a minute, so the device comes back around fast.
  • One stick, one button, no reading. A 6 year old and a grandparent learn each game the same way, by watching.
  • A team game. In Lantern Parade nobody loses: everyone lights the lanterns together and shares the stars.
  • Ghost runs. Race a recording of the best round played on your device, so a child can chase a parent’s score.
  • Table games. Seven table games to play against a relative.

Tips for a good game night

  • Let the youngest pick first. In Sloth Jam, last place picks the game for the final round, which keeps everyone in it.
  • Keep it short. One match is three rounds. Two or three matches make a full evening for young kids.
  • Mix in a team game. Play Lantern Parade between rounds when tempers run high.
  • Add a screen-free break. Our free coloring pages of the island animals make a good warm-up or wind-down.

What you need

One phone, tablet, Chromebook or computer with a web browser. There is nothing to install, and it is free while in early access. Made for ages 6 and up, and safe by design: no open chat, no online play unless a grown-up turns it on, no loot boxes.
