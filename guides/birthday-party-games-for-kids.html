Party games for a kid’s birthday party

A parent guide · October 2026

Great birthday parties feel busy without feeling rushed. The trick is to make lots of small wins happen in a short time, with very little waiting. This plan uses stations and short matches so every child gets many turns and nobody is stuck on the sidelines. It works in a living room, a basement or a small gym, and it scales from six kids to twelve with the same outline.

Set up three stations

Stations keep the room flowing. Each station runs a simple activity with a clear goal and a short timer. Rotate groups every ten minutes. If you have twelve kids, make three groups of four; if you have fewer, make two groups and a rest corner with water.

  • Movement station. A safe obstacle path, bean bag toss into a laundry basket, or a balloon keep up game. Mark a start and finish with tape.
  • Quiet fun station. Coloring pages and crayons on a table. These free Sloth Jam coloring pages are easy to print.
  • Screen station, optional. A single device on a stand with quick party games everyone can play by turns. Sloth Jam runs in the browser on a phone, tablet or computer and every game lasts one minute, so the device comes back around fast.

Use short, kind games

Short rounds help the party feel fair. With one minute games, a child never waits longer than a song. Pick games with simple controls and no reading needed so new players can jump in by watching. In Sloth Jam, each player uses one stick and one button, and the youngest can copy what they see. There are 64 one minute party games, so you can pick different games for each rotation.

Make turn taking simple

  • Pick the next player before the round starts. Say the order out loud. A paper list works well for a group of six to eight.
  • Use a visible timer. If you do not have one on screen, set a phone timer for one minute. End the round even if a player wants to keep going, then give them another turn when it comes back around.
  • Let the birthday child choose the last game. A small choice at the end feels special and keeps the day about them.

Ideas that do not need screens

  • Treasure hunt with pictures. Hide simple picture cards around the room. Kids bring one card back at a time and trade it for a sticker. No reading needed.
  • Minute to win it mini games. Stack cups, balance a balloon on a plate, or slide a coin across a table to land on a mark. Keep each mini game to sixty seconds.
  • Freeze parade. Put on a playlist. Kids parade around with silly walks, then freeze when the music stops. Short songs mean short waits.

What to do when energy dips

  • Offer a quiet break. Color a page, build a small puzzle, or sit with a picture book. Some kids need a moment away from the group to reset.
  • Switch to a team game. A fully cooperative round calms the room. In Sloth Jam there is a team game called Lantern Parade where nobody loses and everyone shares the stars.
  • Serve water before sugar. A quick sip break fixes a surprising number of problems.

Running the screen station

Place the device where an adult can see it. Show the controls once, then let the players try. Keep each match to three or four rounds so new kids join often. In Sloth Jam, a match is three rounds and the last round counts double, which keeps everyone in it. It is free while in early access, there is nothing to download, and it runs in the browser, so setup takes one minute.

Checklist for the day

  • Make three signs for the station names, plus arrows for the rotation direction.
  • Print coloring pages and set out a big pile of crayons.
  • Test the browser game on your device and turn on do not disturb.
  • Put a tape line on the floor for toss games and mark a safe zone for running.
  • Keep a trash bag, wipes and water in the room so you do not need to leave.
