Games grandparents and kids can play together

A family guide · October 2026

The best mixed age play is easy to read, kind and quick. Grandparents and young kids both enjoy short rounds with clear goals, and both do better when no one is stuck waiting. These tips help two very different players share the same game without stress.

Make the screen easy to read

  • Use the TV when you can. Big targets and bright feedback help everyone. If you use a tablet, sit close and turn up the brightness.
  • Avoid tiny text. Pick games that explain with pictures or a quick example. Reading small text is tiring for everyone.
  • Keep controls simple. One stick and one button let both players focus on the goal, not the controller.

Pick gentle modes

  • Fully cooperative rounds. Work together so nobody loses. In Sloth Jam, Lantern Parade is a team game where everyone lights lanterns and shares the stars.
  • Short two player bouts. A quick table game gives just enough competition without long downtime.
  • Kart races with points for many things. When doors, coins and laps all score, everyone ends with a smile.

Set turn taking rules that stay kind

  • Say the order out loud. A simple left to right order at the start of each match keeps things clear.
  • Let the child pick a game. A small choice can rescue a moment of frustration.
  • End early if needed. Two short matches is plenty. Take a coloring break, then come back.

Games to try

  • Catch and avoid. Catch fruit, avoid the grumpy ones. Everyone understands this in a second.
  • Memory pairs. Twelve cards, six pairs. Talk through the choices together.
  • Drift kart. Take turns on a simple track. Hold the button in a turn, then release for a boost.
