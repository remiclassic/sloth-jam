Family game night on the TV

A parent guide · October 2026

You can put a family game on the TV in three simple ways. Pick the one your home already supports, then choose games that make good TV play: clear controls, big targets, short rounds and easy turn taking. This guide covers casting from a phone, an HDMI cable from a laptop, and using a built in browser on a console or smart TV.

Cast from a phone or tablet

  • Use the cast button. If your TV has Chromecast built in, or you have a Chromecast device, open the game in your phone’s browser and tap the cast icon. iOS can use AirPlay to an Apple TV or a compatible smart TV.
  • Keep the device close. Sit near the TV so the signal stays strong, and plug in a charger for longer play.
  • Pick games with simple inputs. On a phone as the controller, one stick and one button is an easy fit for young kids.

Use a cable from a laptop

  • HDMI is the easy default. Most laptops connect to a TV with one HDMI cable. If you need an adapter, test it the day before.
  • Mirror the display or extend it. Mirroring keeps things simple, and extending lets you keep notes open. Either works for family play.
  • Turn on do not disturb. Mute notifications so a pop up does not cover the game. Full screen the browser for a clean look.

Open the TV or console browser

  • Built in browsers work for simple games. Some smart TVs and game consoles include a web browser. Open slothjam.com and press play.
  • Use a controller. Games that use one stick and one button are easy on a controller. Short games also help when typing is slow.
  • Check the privacy settings. If the device is not yours, sign out and clear history when you finish.

Pick games that suit the couch

  • Short rounds. One minute games mean a turn comes back fast. This keeps a five or six year old in the loop.
  • Big targets and bright feedback. From ten feet away, the game should be easy to read. Avoid tiny text.
  • One stick and one button. Young kids copy what they see. Clear inputs help a grandparent join too.

Set a family flow that works

  • Order the turns. Left to right on the sofa works well. Say it out loud so everyone knows when they go.
  • Let the last place player choose a game. This small rule keeps hope alive, and it helps with sharing.
  • Mix team and solo rounds. If tempers rise, play a fully cooperative round together to reset the mood.

Troubleshooting

  • Lag on wireless cast. Switch to a cable or close apps on the phone. Lower Wi Fi traffic if you can.
  • Sound but no picture. On laptops, check the display output mode and the HDMI input on the TV.
  • Controller not detected. Re plug it and press a button on the home screen first so the browser sees input.
